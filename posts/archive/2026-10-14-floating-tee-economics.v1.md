A floating golf tee in a lake. One dollar a ball. $10,000 if you hole it.

Someone reverse-engineered that business recently: roughly $600k a year from one pontoon. In New Zealand there's a lakeside version doing about $650k. In Hong Kong, LIV Golf had Bryson DeChambeau hitting balls into Victoria Harbour for HSBC.

Same model everywhere. Sell a cheap shot at a big prize. Insure the prize. Put it where people already are.

I love this category because it's simple enough to explain to a 10-year-old and big enough to run on every par 3 on earth.

Three things I've learned building our version in South Africa:

Price the moment, not the odds. An amateur's hole-in-one is roughly a 1 in 12,500 shot. Nobody is buying the odds. They're buying the story they tell at the 19th. R50 is a no-brainer. R500 is a dare. Both sell.

The venue is your channel. Every par 3 is a shop already full of your customers. Zero cost to the club, a share of every swing. That's why clubs say yes.

Underwrite, don't hope. Insured prizes turn a gamble into a product that corporates, charities and schools can run. A school keeps 50% of the swings sold on its day. That's fundraising with a camera on it.
