I can't code. Since March I've shipped 17 things.

A promotions dashboard. 32 branded club storefronts. A WhatsApp agent that takes first contact for a property business at 9pm. A site for a simulator venue. Payment plumbing I own for the first time in my life.

None of it built by a developer.

The honest version of AI for a small business is not a robot running the company. It's this:

The mechanical work got cheap. A 5-year cashflow model used to take days and a finance brain. Now it takes minutes, and the thinking is the expensive part. Which is where it should have been all along.

The rules in your head can be written down. Compliance wording, pricing logic, how a mailer is built. Once it's a rule the machine follows, it can't be forgotten at 11pm by whoever is doing the job.

It's confidently wrong sometimes. If you don't know your business well enough to smell when something's off, you'll ship the mistake. Stay in the room.

The first two weeks were bad. I was asking when I should have been briefing. Same skill I've used on designers and distributors for 15 years, pointed somewhere new.

I now teach this in a 3-hour workshop in Cape Town. Eight people, your own laptop, one real job from your business. You leave with it running, not a folder of notes about AI.

R3,500 a seat. Dates and seats: entrepreneurcoach.co.za/workshop

Launch, learn, repeat. Turns out it works for software too.
